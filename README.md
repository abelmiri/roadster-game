# Roadster

A side-view city runner built with Vite, TypeScript, Three.js, and Rapier 2D, with original synthesized sound effects and English/Farsi interfaces.

By [abelmiri](https://github.com/abelmiri) · [miri1888@gmail.com](mailto:miri1888@gmail.com)

The supplied app artwork is preserved in `assets/branding/roadster-icon.png`. Standard PNG renditions (16, 32, 48, 180, 192, and 512 px), a multi-size favicon, and `public/site.webmanifest` provide the browser, home-screen, and web-app icons. The original artwork is resized without redrawing or cropping.

## Run locally

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Open the URL Vite prints. Development mode shows a small translucent FPS counter at the top, refreshed twice per second. It is omitted from production. For a production bundle, run `npm run build`. Run the simulation checks with `npm test`.

## Controls

| Action | Keyboard | Landscape touch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jump / long jump | Tap or hold Space, Up, or W | Tap or hold Jump |
| Double jump | Press jump once more in the air | Tap Jump again |
| Road suction | Hold Down or S | Hold Suction |
| Ground / air boost | Hold Shift | Hold Boost |
| Air nozzles (airborne only) | Hold Z to lift the nose; X to lift the tail | Hold Front (↶) / Rear (↷) |
| Fire | Hold F | Hold Fire |
| Aim front / rear | Right / D, Left / A; Q toggles | Aim toggle (↔) |
| Choose weapon | G opens/closes Armory; 1–8 equips owned weapons (number row or numpad) | Armory button in the header |
| Pause / resume | P or Escape | Pause button |
| Mute / unmute | M | Sound button |

On landscape touch screens, use the four-arrow button in the header to edit the button layout. Drag Jump, Fire, Boost, Turn gun, Suction, Front nozzle, or Rear nozzle anywhere in the control area and adjust each button's size independently (44–128 px). The selector also lets you reach overlapping or hidden buttons. The eye toggle shows or hides the selected control during play; hidden controls remain as faded, editable previews in the editor. Only the front nozzle is shown by default; the rear nozzle can be enabled in this editor. Every button uses a scalable outline icon and a more transparent background. Save keeps the positions, sizes, and visibility in `roadster-touch-layout`; Cancel discards edits, and Reset restores the default arrangement before saving. Positions adapt to the available screen size and safe areas. Editing pauses an active run and resumes it when finished.

Mobile menus use roughly 35% smaller type and tighter card layouts so the ready, pause, armory, garage, coin guide, and record menus fit small landscape screens. Health and XP form a compact stack at the bottom, with smaller header text. Ammunition and remaining boost appear as circular gauges and localized numbers on the Fire and Boost buttons; the gauges follow custom button positions and sizes. Hiding one of those buttons restores its ordinary meter so the resource remains visible. The coin guide uses concise mobile reward explanations.

The car drives automatically, starting at 60 km/h (16.67 m/s) and building toward 18 m/s. Boost adds 9 m/s to the target speed, accelerates at 18 m/s², and reaches up to 27 m/s (97 km/h). It works on the road and in the air, produces white nozzle smoke, and recharges when released. An exhausted boost needs a button release before it can fire again. Your best distance is stored in this browser.

Finishing a run with a new best score opens a medal dialog to record the driver's name. The score is saved immediately, and the name is shown below the best distance after submission. Reloading before naming restores the pending dialog without paying the record bonus again. The versioned `roadster-progress` save stores the record, name, coin balance, and purchased weapons under a stable key across app builds. Existing `roadster-best` scores are retained, and new records also update that legacy key. Browser data remains local to the same browser profile and site origin.

The same save also retains the last run's distance in its `score` field. Driving checkpoints it at most once per second, with immediate saves when pausing, leaving the page, or finishing a run. After reloading, the ready-screen score is labeled **Last run**; starting a new run resets only that distance and run counters. Saved records, coins, lifetime XP (`roadster-xp`), and mobile button positions, sizes, and visibility (`roadster-touch-layout`) remain intact. Older saves without a score field retain all existing progress and start with a zero last score.

The garage's **Car** tab offers twelve permanent paints for **50 coins each**: blue, pink, red, black, purple, yellow, neon green, silver, orange, turquoise, gold, and burgundy. The original white stays free. Buying a paint unlocks that color for the whole garage and applies it only to the currently selected car. Each car remembers its own color independently through switches, restarts and reloads; using an already unlocked color on another car is free. Color unlocks, per-car selections and the wallet are saved together in `roadster-progress`. Legacy global paint selections migrate only to the previously selected car, while the other cars default to white. The preview and in-game body share each paint's shaded palette, including after damage, repairs, and restarts.

Purple mystery boxes appear only after 300 meters of driving, on clear sections of road, either resting on the ground or hovering on two small jet nozzles. Their density is reduced by 20% using a deterministic 80% keep chance for otherwise eligible placements after the opening distance. Both emit a larger, curved purple smoke plume that drifts with the wind. Shooting a ground box folds its four hinged walls outward and stops the smoke; shooting an airborne box ignites its two jets at varied intervals and sends it upward. These animations replace the explosion, without explosion sound or slow motion, and pause/reset with gameplay. The shot immediately grants exactly one random prize; driving through it does not open it. Coin prizes are **20 / 50 / 100 / 200**, with probabilities **28% / 20% / 10% / 4%**. A **20-second magnetic shield** and **20-second unlimited boost** each have a **12%** chance. XP prizes are **100 / 250 / 500**, with probabilities **8% / 4% / 2%**. The shield prevents police and obstacle damage to both armor and hull, remains visibly lit, and blinks during its final three seconds. Unlimited boost works with an empty tank and does not consume the existing reserve. The HUD shows both timers and an infinity symbol on boost. Another copy refreshes its own power to 20 seconds; the powers can run together. Timers and smoke pause with gameplay, powers clear on a wreck or restart, and opened boxes cannot reappear when their chunks reload. Coin and XP counters retain every prize across skipped display frames and persist through the existing progress systems.

Coins are earned once for each 200-meter milestone in a run (**10 coins**), completed loop (**5 coins**), full airborne flip or backflip (**5 base coins**), and destroyed police car (**10 coins**). Multiple flips in the same jump multiply that jump's total flip reward by its flip count: **5 × n²**, giving **5 / 20 / 45 / 80 coins** for one through four flips. Each completed turn immediately credits the difference, and landing or entering a magnetic loop resets the multiplier. Skipped display frames retain every reward; revisiting a distance or target cannot pay again. At the end of a strictly better run, a score over 1,000 meters earns **10 bonus coins**, or over 2,000 meters earns **25 bonus coins**, with only the higher tier paid. Bonuses are separate from distance rewards. The HUD and armory show the wallet balance, and the game-over summary shows coins earned that run.

Six additional distance bonuses are paid once per run: **100 coins at 1,500 m**, **250 at 3,000 m**, **500 at 4,500 m**, **750 at 6,000 m**, **1,000 at 7,500 m**, and **1,500 at 10,000 m**. These add 4,100 bonus coins to the existing regular distance rewards; reaching 10,000 m earns 4,600 coins from distance alone. The same six milestones also award **150 / 300 / 600 / 1,000 / 1,500 / 2,000 XP**, totaling **5,550 XP** at 10,000 m. The top progress bar marks all six goals, shows both the coin and XP reward for the next goal, and displays all coins and XP earned during the current run. The earning guide lists both rewards for every goal. Distance XP is saved in `roadster-xp`, contributes to levels and weapon eligibility, and appears in the game-over XP total. Each goal pays once per run; skipped frames retain all crossed rewards, reversing never pays twice, and wreck movement awards no further XP. Its route progress caps at 10,000 m while ordinary 200-meter earnings continue beyond that point. A new run resets the bar and goal claims while retaining the wallet.

The main ready/pause/game-over menu also displays the wallet. Clicking the balance there, in the HUD, or in the armory opens a bilingual coin guide with the six distance bonuses and explanations of regular driving, loops, destroyed patrols, and record rewards. Its **Buy coins** tab states that coin purchases are currently unavailable. Opening the guide pauses active driving; closing it restores the underlying menu or resumes the run that opened it, without leaving held controls active.

The car starts with 100% health. Each obstacle or police impact takes 25% and plays a metallic crash sound. Nonfatal hits freeze the scene for 500 ms, followed by 1.5 seconds of flashing protection during driving time. One collision counts once even if multiple wheels and the chassis touch. Body damage progresses through 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%: scratches, dents, branching cracks, a crumpled hood, and finally a darkened wreck emitting black engine smoke. Ramming a barrier, pulse obstacle, or drone shatters it into forward-moving fragments and disables its collision immediately, including while the car is protected. Ramming grants no weapon XP; shattered obstacles stay gone for that run.

The fatal hit opens the restart menu immediately over a blurred scene, without an impact freeze. Over two real seconds, the background camera eases to a persistent 1.5× zoom (50% closer) centered on the captured fatal-crash position. The base framing stays fixed so nearby loops cannot prolong the zoom. The focus stays fixed while the wreck moves and makes further contacts; restarting clears the zoom. Restart controls stay locked for three real seconds while the wreck plays; a localized countdown disables the restart button, then a fresh key press or click is required. Early or held input cannot queue a restart. The chassis and both wheels separate into independent Rapier bodies, retaining momentum and colliding with solid roads and remaining obstacles. This scene continues at 12.5% simulation speed behind the menu, half its previous 25% speed; driving, combat rewards, and distance scoring have ended. The player's black engine smoke emits twice as many puffs per simulation second during game over, increasing its density without changing police smoke. Falls and roof-first landings also produce a loose wreck. Restart restores the undamaged body, attached wheels, full health, and clean smoke/debris pools.

After collision hit stop, simulation speed eases from zero to normal over 800 ms. Weapon explosions never freeze the scene: speed eases down to 25% over 200 ms, then returns to normal over 1000 ms. Further explosions begin from the current time scale without snapping back to full speed. Physics, camera movement, smoke, fragments, and sparks share this time scale. Each obstacle or police collision throws a large shower of hot yellow sparks, including impacts during flashing protection. The particles fall under gravity, bounce and skid along the road, cool toward orange, and fade over 1.3–2.4 seconds. Crashed chassis contacts also produce sparks behind the restart menu. Gaps let particles fall freely.

After 12 seconds without an active boost, red/blue police lights begin to appear behind the car; after 14 seconds the patrol closes the gap at 2.2 m/s. Each new chase starts 30 meters behind, including after loops taken while the patrol was absent. The later warning and slower closing rate give roughly four more seconds before contact. Boost pushes it back. The warning, edge lights, and siren grow as it approaches. Police contact at road height uses the same health and protection rules as obstacles. Starting cruise speed is 60 km/h.

The black-and-white wedge patrol has a POLICE door marking, shield/star badge, eight-spoke wheels, front push bar, and blue/red roof lights. It drives forward along the ordinary road while the player circles a loop. Whenever it passes the player, including during or after an abandoned loop, it brakes smoothly to 35% of the player's speed (limited to 4–8 m/s). It stays slow until the player has overtaken it with at least eight meters of clearance, then resumes pursuit. Police motion stays continuous through these transitions. Any actual collision while overtaking costs 25% health; a completed lap itself awards XP without automatic damage. After a hit, the patrol gives the player room to escape.

Optional circular roads appear with procedural spacing of 200–400 meters, with joins shifted when needed to clear a ramp's approach and landing. Hold suction at the entrance and throughout a full lap to take the loop; otherwise continue along the ordinary road. Releasing suction detaches the car and forfeits that lap's reward. Entries and exits have no hazards or gaps. Every completed loop earns **25 XP**, once only. Destroying any road hazard with a weapon earns **10 XP**; destroying a patrol earns **20 XP**. Partial hits and ramming award no combat XP. Each destroyed target pays once, including splash damage or streamed chunks revisited near loops; a replacement patrol is a new target. Rewards appear with their source, count toward the run total and weapon unlocks, and are saved in `roadster-xp`. Each 100 XP advances a level. XP persists across crashes, restarts, and reloads.

After 1,000 meters, 8–9.5 m launch ramps appear at random intervals of **400–800 meters**, measured from one ramp's start to the next. The first interval starts at the 1,000 m unlock point. Spacing uses the track's deterministic procedural randomness so streamed chunks and revisited sections keep the same ramps. Their smooth climbs and short crests launch the car naturally, with boost producing longer flights. The visible road, tire contacts, and colliders share the same ramp profile across chunk boundaries. Each ramp reserves a clear approach and landing without gaps, obstacles, or magnetic loop joins.

A glowing booster strip with forward chevrons sits near each ramp's takeoff edge. Driving a grounded tire across it gives an immediate **7 m/s** forward impulse along the road tangent, with takeoff spin damped for a stable launch. The extra speed eases away, and boost exhaust marks the kick without spending the player's reserve. Each pad triggers once per run, including when both axles cross it or its chunk reloads; flying above a pad does not activate it.

Use the language button to switch between English and فارسی. The Farsi interface uses natural, friendly written copy, the bundled Vazirmatn variable font, right-to-left text, and Persian digits. Physical key names such as Space, Shift, P, and Esc stay in English. Health and boost are at the top; XP and speed are at the bottom. The font's SIL Open Font License is included in `public/fonts/OFL-Vazirmatn.txt`. Language and mute preferences are saved locally. Sound starts after your first interaction and fades when the game is paused or loses focus.

Desktop HUD readouts and meters retain their 25% enlargement; mobile uses the compact layout described above. Health loss has a short red kick and a delayed trail showing the lost segment; recovery glows green. Air use and refill have distinct colors, with a quiet refill sweep. Speed digits ease toward the current speed, score milestones and new records pulse, and ammunition reacts to firing. XP gains glow, with a gold level-up pulse and a fill-to-full/reset sequence at level boundaries. Meter values remain exact for accessibility. Feedback is throttled during continuous changes, uses transform animations, and respects reduced-motion preferences.

## Weapons

The roof turret turns through 180° before firing in the new direction. Turning takes 550–950 ms depending on the weapon and uses simulation time, so pause and impact freeze preserve it. Small automatic elevation adjustments help target road hazards and drones. Bullets use swept collision checks; terrain blocks shots. Weapon recoil is visual and cannot upset the suspension.

Lifetime weapon unlocks require 200 / 500 / 900 / 1,400 / 2,000 / 2,800 / 3,800 XP after the starter, and XP is never spent. The starter Machine Gun is free (0 XP, 80 rounds). Each later gun requires XP and a one-time coin purchase: Twin Gun (200 XP, 1,000 coins, 100 rounds), Gatling Gun (500 XP, 2,000 coins, 150 rounds), Cannon (900 XP, 3,000 coins, 16 rounds), Rocket Launcher (1,400 XP, 4,000 coins, 12 rounds), Missile Launcher (2,000 XP, 5,000 coins, 10 rounds), Laser Gun (2,800 XP, 6,000 coins, 50 charges), and Plasma Cannon (3,800 XP, 7,000 coins, 18 rounds). Twin volleys spend two rounds. Heavy weapons deal splash damage, missiles track their target, and the laser hits instantly. Models, icons, projectiles, muzzle flashes, impacts, and firing sounds are original.

The armory pauses an active run and resumes it on closing. A purchase debits coins and saves ownership together; bought weapons can be equipped repeatedly at no further cost. Number shortcuts only equip owned weapons and never spend coins. Each weapon retains its own remaining ammunition for that run, so switching cannot refill it. A new run refills all weapons. Selection persists in `roadster-weapon`; corrupt, unavailable, unowned, or XP-locked saved selections fall back to the Machine Gun.

Every owned weapon has five upgrade levels. Each level adds 20% of the base firing rate, hit damage, and ammunition capacity; level five doubles all three. Capacity rounds up to whole volleys. The next upgrade costs `ceil(max(1,000, purchase price) × 0.5 × 1.25^current level)` coins, so the first upgrade starts at half the purchase price and each later price rises by a factor of 1.25. Prices round up to whole coins. The free starter uses a 1,000-coin basis, making its five upgrades cost 500, 625, 782, 977, and 1,221 coins. Upgrade levels and their coin debit are saved together in the existing `roadster-progress` record; older saves start at level zero without losing records, coins, or ownership.

Before purchase, each weapon card also shows its required player level and the current player level, alongside the existing XP and coin conditions. The current weapon thresholds map to player levels 1 / 3 / 6 / 10 / 15 / 21 / 29 / 39 using 100 XP per level, starting at level 1. The armory shows five level markers, current combat stats, the next level's stats, and a separate upgrade button for each owned gun. Insufficient funds and fully upgraded weapons disable that button. Upgrades apply immediately, including to unequipped guns, and add only the newly unlocked ammunition slots, preserving rounds already spent. Switching and reapplying a level cannot refill ammunition. New runs and reloads start at the saved upgraded capacity. Projectiles already in flight retain the damage they had at launch.

The same menu has a **Car** tab with four selectable cars: Roadster, Samandster (a long four-door sedan), Tibaster (a compact hatchback), and Peugester (a rounded hatch with swept windows and teardrop headlights), styled from the supplied references. Each unowned car costs **100 coins**, with a confirmation before payment. The starter Roadster remains owned. Vehicle ownership and selection persist in `roadster-progress`; older saves also retain their previously selected car. Switching owned cars is free. The color palette and the three four-level upgrade tracks are shared across the garage, while each car saves its own chosen paint. A health, boost reserve or boost power upgrade applies to every car in every color, including cars bought later, with no second purchase. Selecting a car changes the actual driving model and its matching preview without refilling ammunition, fuel, health, or armor. Taller cabins also raise the physical firing origin, visible turret and roof armor together. Health/armor starts at 4,000 coins (then 6,000 / 9,000 / 13,500). Boost reserve and boost power each start at 3,000 coins (then 4,500 / 6,750 / 10,125). Purchases debit and save atomically in the existing `roadster-progress` data, preserving earlier records, coins, weapons, and weapon upgrades. Tabs support arrow keys and Home/End; focus stays in the visible panel and number shortcuts only select weapons in the Weapons tab.

Vehicle upgrades additionally require player XP levels: health stages need **50 / 100 / 150 / 200**, and each boost track needs **25 / 50 / 75 / 100**. These are player levels, calculated by the existing 100-XP-per-level progression. The garage shows the next required level and the current player level. Both the UI and the purchase logic enforce the requirement; XP is never consumed. Previously purchased vehicle levels remain active even if an older save is below the new level requirement.

Each health upgrade adds 25 maximum health and one cumulative armor component matching the reference: front guard, bolted door plate, roof cap, and rear guard. The unarmored hull retains 100 health; four plates raise total health to 200. An accepted collision drops one installed plate and absorbs that hit before hull damage begins. Rear police contacts preferentially remove the rear guard. Protection prevents duplicate plate loss from overlapping contacts. Lost pieces fall, spin, bounce on roads, and expire in a four-piece visual pool; they cause no further damage or rewards. The HUD adds health segments for the purchased capacity, and body dents follow hull damage. New runs restore all purchased plates. Mid-run health upgrades add only new plates and preserve existing hull damage and lost plates; buying upgrades after defeat applies them to the next run.

Boost reserve adds 25% of the base tank per level, reaching twice the duration at level four; fuel use and refill scale with the larger capacity. Boost power adds 20% of the base acceleration and speed bonus per level, reaching 1.8× at level four. The original 9 m/s boost speed bonus therefore reaches 16.2 m/s, and the boosted cruise limit rises accordingly. Expanding reserve adds only the newly unlocked fuel space; reapplying a saved level cannot refill fuel. The garage shows current and next-level stats and disables unavailable or completed upgrades.

Road hazards take 2–4 damage to destroy and stop colliding once destroyed. Weapon destruction scatters fragments in all directions, including from the patrol, with continuous explosion slow motion independent of collision damage and protection. Bullet sparks launch in a cone opposite the actual incoming shot direction, including rear fire and laser beams. Surviving police hits also spark; terrain impacts produce only seven smaller sparks. Terrain hit points are refined along the swept shot and gaps do not block fire.

The patrol has 12 combat health; destroying it disables contact damage and releases its crumpled body and both wheels into independent terrain physics, with black engine smoke following the body. Wreck parts do not cause player damage or award extra XP. There is at most one police wreck, removed after ten simulation seconds or once far behind the player. A replacement gets eight seconds before its new warning timer starts and approaches from behind. Shooting does not consume boost or change the player's health rules, and loops still award 25 XP.

## Missions and purchase confirmations

The mission board (header, pause menu, or **J**) offers a persistent campaign of **100 unique goals**, with three active missions at a time. It covers directional flips, speed holds, night driving, fatal roof contact, shooting and ramming hazards, police, distinct emptied guns, loot boxes, loops, jumps, flight, boost, clean driving and lifetime milestones. Tiers unlock in order within each family; equipment-dependent missions wait until enough weapons are owned. A completed ID is archived permanently and never assigned again. Replacement slots are filled on the next run, preventing earlier actions from completing newly assigned goals.

Finished goals stay in their active slots with a **Collect** button until their rewards are claimed. Uncollected goals survive reloads and new runs without resetting or unlocking replacements. Collect credits coins and mission points atomically with the completed ID and history, once only; the freed slot receives its replacement on the next run. Unfinished per-run goals reset when a new run starts but retain their best attempt. Lifetime totals, active assignments and checkpoints survive reloads; pauses flush progress and ordinary checkpoints are throttled to one second. Mission points are separate from player XP. Simulation counters include final contacts and exclude paused time and wreck animation.

All shop menus distinguish availability and ownership with icons and text: unavailable items show a lock and the missing coin/level condition; affordable unowned items show a gold shopping badge; owned items show a teal check, with a stronger filled badge for the current selection. Purchased upgrade stages stay marked as owned even when their next stage is locked. Completed tracks show a check instead of a lock. Disabled controls remain inactive, and affordable weapon prices stay visible on mobile.

Every paid weapon purchase, weapon upgrade, vehicle purchase, shared vehicle upgrade and paint purchase opens a confirmation dialog showing the item, changed stats, exact price, current wallet and remaining balance. Cancel is initially focused; Escape cancels, focus is trapped and underlying controls are inert. Confirmation rechecks price, upgrade stage, XP, ownership and funds before a single debit. Switching owned weapons, colors or car models remains free. Paint quotes capture the intended car and reject confirmation if that car changes before payment.

## Project structure

- `src/game/track.ts` generates deterministic road chunks, gaps, hazards, and district progress. Rendering and physics share its road profile.
- `src/game/pursuit.ts` controls the police warning, chase distance, and boost escape timing.
- `src/game/policeWreck.ts` simulates the destroyed patrol's independent body and wheels.
- `src/game/experience.ts` awards loop XP, deduplicates rewards, and persists level progress.
- `src/game/coinRewards.ts` defines the six additional distance coin/XP bonuses and route progress. `src/coins.css` styles the top reward bar, clickable menu wallets, and the earning/purchase information dialog.
- `src/game/playerProgress.ts` persists records, pending record names, coins, weapon ownership, and vehicle/weapon upgrades, migrates legacy best scores, and deduplicates run rewards and purchases. `src/progress.css` styles the wallet, prices, garage tabs, and record dialog.
- `src/game/carUpgrades.ts` defines the three four-level vehicle upgrade tracks, their prices, and shared stat multipliers. `src/game/armorParts.ts` shares the four reference silhouettes between the garage preview and in-game armor; `carArmor.ts` attaches and drops the matching 3D plates, and `carGarageIcon.ts` draws the four matching previews. `carModels.ts` defines saved model IDs and roof offsets; `vehicleModels.ts` builds the new sedan and hatchback shells with the existing open fenders and jet positions.
- `src/game/physics.ts` owns the fixed-step Rapier simulation, controls, and collision rules.
- `src/game/wheelContact.ts` integrates independent tire contacts, suspension forces, impact energy loss, chassis momentum, and finite road suction.
- `src/game/damage.ts` controls health, temporary protection, damage stages, hit stop, and the wreck time scale.
- `src/game/carBodyDamage.ts` deforms the body and adds progressive dents, panel fractures, and broken glass only on damage transitions.
- `src/game/vehicle.ts` shares the car dimensions between rendering and tire support.
- `src/game/roadsterModel.ts` shares the wedge silhouette and wheel geometry between the player and patrol car.
- `src/game/suspension.ts` supplies the additional sprung-body motion inside magnetic loops.
- `src/game/audio.ts` synthesizes the motor, road, wind, jump, landing, boost, suction, warning, crash, and interface sounds.
- `src/game/smoke.ts` draws pooled white jet exhaust and rising black smoke from the destroyed engine in one instanced draw. Downward jet emission follows the actual held-jump thrust state. Effects pause with the run, continue during the slow-motion wreck, and reset on restart.
- `src/game/visuals.ts` renders the orthographic 3D scene, parallax city, and district palettes.
- `src/game/terrain.ts` extends the ground to -90 m with continuous procedural rock strata and a separate narrow asphalt cap. Terrain textures use world coordinates across streamed chunk boundaries.
- `src/game/rampBooster.ts` draws the luminous, road-aligned launch pads at ramp edges; their positions are shared with tire-triggered impulses in the physics simulation.
- `src/game/startDepot.ts` turns the start boundary into a neon garage exit with a solid launch apron, tunnel ribs, direction arrows, caution strips, and an industrial retaining wall. Repeated details are instanced; the depot stops drawing once it leaves the camera view.
- `src/game/cityBuildings.ts` batches the skyline and windows into five instanced draws, updating buffers only when buildings wrap.
- `src/game/obstacleDebris.ts` renders at most 192 pooled fragments with radial explosion impulses or forward ramming momentum, gravity, ground bounces, and pause/reset support.
- `src/game/impactSparks.ts` renders up to 512 pooled spark streaks in one draw, with terrain bounces, cooling, and pause/reset support.
- `src/game/impactCamera.ts` widens the ordinary view by 20% and frames nonfatal car collisions with a temporary 1.7× zoom. The game-over scene holds a 1.5× zoom on the captured fatal position. Camera motion continues through hit stop, respects manual pause, and resets on restart; bullet impacts and later wreck contacts cannot retarget the final shot.
- `src/game/drivingCamera.ts` smoothly widens the driving view at high speed and anticipates tall jumps, lifting the framing to keep both the car and road visible. It eases back after slowing or landing, preserves loop framing, pauses with gameplay, and holds its base view throughout the fatal crash transition.
- `src/game/backdropBlur.ts` softly blurs the half-resolution city and adds slow, theme-colored atmospheric haze before drawing the sharp road, car, hazards, police, and loops.
- `src/game/policeVisual.ts` draws the original patrol car and alternating red/blue beacons.
- `src/game/weapons.ts` defines eight XP unlocks, per-run ammunition, turret rotation, projectiles, targeting, and combat hits.
- `src/game/weaponVisual.ts` draws the eight roof turrets and pooled projectile/impact effects; `weaponIcon.ts` supplies armory silhouettes.
- `src/main.ts` owns the game loop, input, overlays, HUD, pause, and local best score.
- `src/mobile.css` compacts mobile menus and headers, stacks health with XP, and puts ammunition and boost rings on the editable touch buttons.
- `src/game/hudFeedback.ts` animates changes to HUD values, smooths speed digits, and bounds animation work per indicator; `src/hud.css` contains the 1.25× indicator sizing and responsive meter styling.
- `src/i18n.ts` contains both languages and localized number formatting.

The game runs at a fixed 120 Hz physics step and interpolates the visible car between steps. A wider orthographic view shows more of the road, while the city is blurred separately from gameplay objects. Rendering uses a three-million-pixel budget with a 1.5× pixel-ratio cap, reducing to 1.8 million pixels after sustained slow frames. Camera projection updates are skipped when unchanged. The HUD only writes changed values; the fixed-step loop checks lightweight impact state instead of building extra render snapshots. Contact samples reuse storage and sample height/slope together, and unloaded suspension stops skip redundant solver passes. Hit stops and speed recovery use wall-clock time independently of the capped simulation timestep. The recovery curve is integrated analytically for consistent timing at different refresh rates; manual pause preserves freeze, recovery, and protection time. The impact pose is held until the first resumed physics step to avoid a backward interpolation jump.

Ordinary camera framing places its center 32.4% of the viewport width ahead of the car, increasing the previous look-ahead offset by 20% to show more upcoming road. Loop framing and the final crash focus keep their dedicated targets. The extended ground prevents its lower boundary appearing in the wider view while retaining real gaps. Subtle stone grain, fractures, and strata are generated in the material without texture uploads. Drifting haze softens the city colors and contrast in the existing background pass; gameplay objects are drawn afterward and stay clear.

The road emerges from a launch garage behind the starting car. Its level apron extends from -64 m to the ordinary road at zero, with matching visual and physical surfaces. A deep concrete retaining wall and service structures replace the exposed cut at the beginning. Detached wheels and wrecks can land on the apron, while the normal start position and first challenges stay the same.

On the ordinary road, the chassis is a free body supported by two independent spring-and-damper tire contacts. Tire clearance follows the continuous surface normal. Assistance also yields to the first loaded tire on landing. The body is never snapped to a supported height or road tangent.
